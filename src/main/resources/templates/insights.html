How the forecast works

Usage per day is modelled as a + b·HDD + c·CDD, where heating/cooling degree days measure how far each day's mean temperature was below/above 18.3 °C (65 °F). It's fitted with ridge regression on your past bills and the daily weather for your city (Open-Meteo / ERA5). For the next period, the weather is taken as the average of the same dates over the previous three years. Cost is then fixed + rate·usage, fitted on your recent bills, so fixed monthly charges aren't mistaken for usage. With fewer than 6 weather-matched bills, the model falls back to the median of recent bills.

Backtest error is the mean absolute percentage error when each of the last few bills is predicted using only earlier bills (and the weather that actually happened), compared with the naive guess "same cost per day as the last bill". Green means the model beats naive.

How anomalies are detected

For each bill, a model is fitted on earlier bills only and predicts usage per day for that period's weather. The gap is scored with a robust z-score, (actual − expected) / (1.4826 × MAD of past residuals). A bill is flagged when z ≥ 3.5 and usage is at least 25% above expected, so small, statistically odd wobbles don't raise alarms. Water spikes are labelled as possible leaks. A price-per-unit jump of 20%+ with normal usage is reported as a rate increase.
